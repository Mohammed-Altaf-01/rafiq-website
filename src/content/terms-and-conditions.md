## 4. The Accessibility permission

Rafiq's blocking depends on Android's Accessibility Service, and we take that responsibility seriously.

**What it is for.** Rafiq uses Accessibility to detect adult content (by reading URLs in your browser and text in search fields), to detect short-video feeds like YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels, and to know which app is in the foreground so it can enforce your time limits.

**What it reads.** The URL in the address bar, text in search fields, the on-screen structure that identifies a Shorts or Reels feed, and which app is open. It ignores password fields and does not read your messages, emails, or page content.

**What it never does.** Rafiq never stores, logs, or transmits any URL, search term, or screen text. Matching happens in memory on your device and the text is discarded immediately. If a block fires, only its type and time are recorded — never what triggered it.

**Your control.** Rafiq will not block anything unless you enable Accessibility, and you can turn it off at any time in Android Settings. When it is off, blocking stops and Rafiq will simply invite you to turn it back on.

## 5. How blocking works

- **Free tier** — blocks still appear, but they are *soft*: after a brief pause you can choose to continue.
- **Premium and trial** — blocks are *strict*: adult-content blocks hold for a timer you set, app-limit blocks hold until midnight, and short-video blocks require you to leave the feed. An optional PIN adds friction to bypassing them.

Rafiq never permanently locks you out. Every block eventually offers a way forward — a timer, an exit, or the next day — which protects you against false positives and honors your autonomy.

Blocking relies on curated lists and heuristics. We cannot guarantee that every harmful thing is caught or that there are never false positives, and detection for Shorts and Reels can break when those apps change (we push fixes without needing an app update). Rafiq works offline using a bundled list; updates need a connection.
